The Full Silhouette.

From The Heart To The Hands — Inside Dolce & Gabbana - Milano

Each look is presented as a complete composition, where structure, styling, and ornament are treated as equally important. What emerges is not just a garment, but a fully realised image shaped through proportion, texture, and detail.

Dolce & Gabbana's visual language is rooted in contrast, tradition and spectacle, discipline and excess. Each piece reflects a deeply Italian narrative, where craftsmanship is not hidden but emphasized, and ornament becomes a form of storytelling. The garments do not simply dress the body; they construct identity, drawing from history, religion, and theatre to create something both timeless and deliberately exaggerated.

Venice Carnival.

Each winter, Venice shifts from a quiet, fog-covered city into a world of masquerade, ornament, and theatrical fashion. Carnival transforms the canals into a living spectacle where craftsmanship, fantasy, and identity blur together. I experienced that transformation firsthand by participating in Venice Carnival myself, moving from observer to part of the spectacle and seeing how costume changes the way the city, its people, and its traditions are experienced.

The Art of More.

Camp: Notes on Fashion · The Costume Institute

Camp is fashion at its most theatrical — exaggerated, ironic, playful, and completely self-aware. I photographed the exhibition as a study in scale, colour, humour, and absurdity: silhouettes turned into characters and garments that deliberately refuse restraint.

What stayed with me most was the seriousness given to humour. Here, excess becomes its own design language, and fashion is allowed to be intelligent without ever becoming too precious.

Future Fashion.

Pierre Cardin: Future Fashion

At the Brooklyn Museum, the mood shifts from theatrical excess toward futurism. Pierre Cardin imagined clothing through geometry, movement, technology, and the visual language of the Space Age.

Seen together, the two exhibitions offered completely different ideas of fantasy: one rooted in humour and exaggeration, the other in optimism about what fashion could become.
